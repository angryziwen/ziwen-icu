# A Letter to BYD's Supervisors and Managers：Push People to the Edge and Someone Will Die

!!! warning "Content note"

    This article discusses workplace pressure, depression and suicide. If you or someone you know is in crisis, please contact a professional helpline first: China's national psychological assistance hotline **12356**, the Beijing Suicide Research and Prevention Center on **010-82951332**, or emergency services on **110** / **120**. Outside China, contact your local emergency number.

---

**To the supervisors and R&D managers:**

Today I won't talk about "organisational optimisation" or "cost-cutting KPIs". I'll say something more direct:

Behind that performance sheet in your hand, behind "we're not paying the profit bonus for now", behind "let's see how we can trim your headcount" — is a living person. Someone with a mortgage, a child, parents, a project they've worked on for three years, and a body that still hasn't recovered from a late-night deployment.

The profit bonus can vanish just like that. The performance rating is inexplicably marked down. An R&D engineer works and works, and is slowly marginalised. Then comes the line: "If you don't want to do this, you can leave."

You think you're "cutting costs". Some people hear something else:

**"I've been lied to. I've been abandoned. My whole family is finished with me."**

Don't laugh. Don't tell yourself that "workers are clear-eyed these days".

When it gets to that point, clarity is useless. Shame, a sense of debt, the feeling of being stabbed in the back — they drag a person into a black hole.

It's not as if this industry has never seen it before: a suicide at a desk, a final goodbye on WeChat Moments, family members storming the company, public opinion exploding overnight.

When that day comes, can you, the direct supervisor, escape it?

**Arbitration is coming:** withheld bonuses, unilateral pay cuts, procedurally flawed decisions — the company pays up. Articles 38 and 46 of the Labour Contract Law are not there for decoration. If wages are not paid in full, an employee forced to resign is still entitled to economic compensation.

**The courts are coming:** a poor performance rating does not equal incompetence; being ranked last does not mean you can be laid off; a profit bonus that was promised must be honoured. Court cases in Chongqing, Guizhou and Xiangyang are already a rebuke to the idea that "I can withhold whatever I want and cut whoever I want".

**Public opinion is coming:** R&D groups, Maimai, Xiaohongshu, WeChat public accounts — it takes half a day to turn "a certain factory's supervisor pushed someone to death" into a living case study.

**If someone actually dies:** your name will be pinned to it for the rest of your life. "I was only following orders" — that sentence won't save you.

I know you are caught in the middle too: the top wants cost cuts, the bottom wants to keep their jobs. You don't control the bonus pool, and you can't stop the reorganisation.

But "caught in the middle" is not a reason to become an accomplice.

## At the very least, don't do these stupid things

**1. Don't use the profit bonus as a punishment** — an incentive you promised shouldn't become "don't pay it if they don't obey".

**2. Don't change the performance criteria at the last minute** — an A last year, a sudden C this year. Employees aren't stupid, and arbitrators aren't blind.

**3. Don't use cold violence to force people out** — no work assigned, messages ignored, excluded from meetings, public humiliation. That's a soft layoff — the cruellest and most unlawful way to do it.

**4. Don't tell R&D engineers "you were always going to be replaced by AI"** — he fixed a production incident at 3 a.m., and you erase it with one sentence. Wouldn't that drive you insane?

**5. If you really have to lay people off, do it with dignity** — give notice in advance, follow the law, pay the compensation in full, make a real effort to reassign people, and let them say goodbye.

This isn't mercy. It's a safety net.

Management is not "writing people off as a cost".

Everyone who stays is watching how you treat the people you laid off, cut pay for, or stopped bonuses for.

If everyone who stays learns one thing—

**"At this company, doing good work gets you bitten; the honest ones die first."**

Then the R&D system will rot from the inside, without a competitor having to lift a finger.

## So, while we're on this subject, let me say something harsh

**Before you stop the profit bonus, ask yourself:** is this money worth trading for a life, a family, a lawsuit, and the trust of the entire R&D organisation?

**Before you downgrade someone's performance, ask yourself:** would I dare put this standard in front of an arbitration tribunal? Would I dare post it in the group chat?

**Before you lay someone off, ask yourself:** if the person this happened to were my brother, my classmate, or me twenty years from now, what would I want my supervisor to do?

Managers, we all lead troops.

What a leader fears most is not a hard battle, but pushing his brothers until they have no way back.

When a person has no way back, no one will carry the work; when no one carries the work, what gets destroyed in the end is our own position, our conscience, and the rest of our lives.

**Cutting costs is understandable. Passing the buck is not.**

**Efficiency is fine. Doing evil is not.**

If the top wants results, we can hand in a plan.

But don't fill the boss's spreadsheet with someone else's breakdown.

## Remember

The company will change CEOs. The stock will fall back. Next year's quarterly targets will be rewritten.

But a person you broke, and the lamp at home that kept waiting for him — those cannot be rewritten. (Or maybe you'll be the one taken to the afterlife.)

Don't wait until the day it happens to post a "solemn notice" in the group chat.

By then, no one will believe your "we regret".

— To the supervisors and R&D managers who still treat people as people.
